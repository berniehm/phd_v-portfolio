# Project objectives — research portfolio tracker

**Repo:** `phd-portfolio` · **Owner:** Bernadette
**Spec:** `BUILD_BRIEF_portfolio_site.md` · **Content:** `paper_register_v2.md`
**Shape:** four milestones. Each one ships something that works and can be described to someone else.

This is a real tool with a real user, which is the thing most portfolio projects are missing. Build it as one.

---

## How to work

Set the four milestones below in GitHub, file the issues under them, close them as you go. The commit history and the closed issues *are* part of the deliverable — people who hire frontend developers read them, and a clean trail of small commits with real messages says more than any single feature.

**The brief is a starting point, not a boundary.** The decisions in it are recommendations with reasoning attached; where you see a better way, take it and write down why. Milestone 4 asks for exactly that. The one thing worth holding to is the scope note at the end — not because the ideas are bad, but because a tool that grows faster than it gets used stops getting used.

## Milestone 3 — Ready queue and blocked ledger
*Ships: the feature that makes it worth using. This is the interesting one.*

The problem: Victor's advisor says *focus on X*. X is waiting on someone else. Rather than sit idle, he needs to know instantly what else is available. That is a computed view, not a page.

- [ ] `needs` field on `Task` — `'none'`, or the person or artefact it waits on
- [ ] **Ready queue** as the default view when nothing is selected: every task with `needs: 'none'` whose parent has no unresolved blocker, sorted by leverage
- [ ] **Blocked ledger**: every blocked item, what it waits on, who holds it, how many days
- [ ] Ageing counters throughout, from `updated`
- [ ] Empty state names the oldest unmoved item
- [ ] `debt` field and `DebtList` — unresolved count on the card, section in the panel
- [ ] Sixth filter: Debt

**Done when** the landing view answers "what can I start right now" without Victor opening a single card.

Worth knowing why this matters: the blocked ledger is the same data pointed at a different audience. When the advisor asks why something hasn't moved, it turns *why haven't you* into *here is where the queue is jammed, and here is what I did instead*.

- [ ] `CASE_STUDY.md` — 600–900 words, the link for a CV

### On the case study

This is the highest-value hour in the project. Cover:

- **The problem in one paragraph.** A researcher with ten concurrent papers who is regularly blocked on other people and needs to know what he can do instead.
- **The constraints.** Two users, one read-only. Unpublished research, so it cannot be public. Content updated a few times a week by the people who own the repo.
- **The decisions, with reasoning.** Static data file rather than a database — version history, diffs, rollback, no infrastructure. Cloudflare Access rather than a login — auth in the browser is decorative, and the least secure part of any site is the login someone wrote themselves. Links out rather than storage — the tool indexes, it does not hold. A LaTeX repo rather than an in-app editor, because rebuilding cross-references, bibliography and equation numbering would land somewhere worse than the tool that already exists.
- **What you added.** The brief is a starting point, not a boundary. Anything you change, simplify or design better than specified belongs here — with the reasoning. That is the part that shows judgement rather than compliance, and it is the part an employer is actually reading for.
- **The one hard part.** The ready queue — turning a list of tasks and dependencies into a single answer to "what can I do right now".

Most portfolios show what was built. Far fewer show why it was built that way, and which paths were considered and set aside.

## Scope

Milestones 1 and 2 are a weekend. Three is a weekend. Four is an afternoon plus the writing.

Anything not in the brief goes in a `FUTURE.md` file rather than into the build. A tracker that grows into a platform becomes a tracker nobody updates, and a stale tracker is worse than none — it looks authoritative while being wrong.
