## What changed, and why it matters

### Paper 3 just fixed your thesis narrative

You described it as equatorial atmospheric winds acting on the ocean, and the ocean then driving the atmospheric response. That is not a digression. **It is Paper 1's question in a different dynamical regime.**

Paper 1 asks whether midlatitude North Atlantic SST forces the circulation above it. Paper 3 asks how that two-way exchange works at the equator, where the dynamics are cleaner and the β-plane makes the wave response tractable. Same physics, different latitude — and the equatorial case is where the mechanism can be seen rather than inferred statistically.

This matters because the framing was a known loose end — the chapter was carried as topically distant and kept in on Assaf's say-so. Under this description it stops being a chapter that needs defending: the thesis is ocean–atmosphere coupling across latitudes and timescales, and Paper 3 is the mechanistic chapter the statistical chapters point at.

Worth putting to Assaf in those words. He has been carrying the justification for you.

### Paper 4 has a job in the thesis

It is not an orphan methods paper. It is *why Paper 1 is a lead-lagged composite study rather than a causal one.* A thesis that changes methods between chapters has to say why, and Paper 4 is that answer written up properly instead of buried in a footnote.

That argues for a real chapter or a substantial appendix rather than an external output. It does not contradict Assaf's cap — his constraint was on scope, not on where it lands.

### Paper 2 is infrastructure for 5 and 6

Not a race condition — a build order. The MedWRs were constructed to see how SST regimes affect the Mediterranean, with R4 and SPEI as the focus. In ICON you switch regimes on and off, watch how that propagates through the South Atlantic, Indian and Pacific basins, and read the effect on the Mediterranean atmosphere through the MedWRs. They are the measuring instrument for 5 and 6.

So the dependency is provenance, not timing. What matters is that the MedWR definition is **frozen once ICON runs begin** — the 20–60°N domain, k = 8, the R4/SPEI focus. Changing it afterwards means re-running experiments, not just redrawing figures. Record it in the debt register as a lock, not a risk.

### Causality is established by intervention, not inference

This is the structural point and it deserves saying plainly.

Paper 1 tried to establish direction statistically and the method failed for a diagnosable reason. Paper 5 establishes it by **intervention** — switching regimes on and off in a controlled model and observing the response. That is a stronger claim than any statistical test on observations could have supported, and it is the standard attribution design.

Which means the thesis does not have a hole where the causality went. It has causality in the right place, made the right way. Paper 1 becomes the observed association, Paper 5 the controlled test, Paper 4 the account of why the observational route does not work. Read in that order the failure is not a setback in the argument — it is the reason the argument is built the way it is.

Ordering between 4 and 5 is therefore free. Either can appear first.

## Does the work still fit the title?

The title was signed in April/May. Paper 1 changed after that. Two points worth having an answer to, neither requiring a title change.

### "Ocean-Forced" — and the direction that survived

The title asserts ocean forcing atmosphere. Paper 1 set out to demonstrate that observationally and could not: the direction that cleared its surrogate null was the opposite one. Taken alone, that is an awkward thing to have in Chapter 1 of a thesis with this title.

It is not taken alone. **Paper 5 establishes ocean forcing by intervention**, which is a stronger demonstration than any observational test could have given. So the title holds — but the route to it now runs through the model, not the statistics, and the introduction has to say so explicitly rather than leaving a reader to notice the gap.

The sequence to state up front: Chapter 1 establishes the observed association and the sub-seasonal lead time. Chapter 4 explains why the observational record cannot settle direction. Chapter 5 settles it by controlled experiment. Chapter 3 shows the coupling is two-way at the equator, where the mechanism is tractable.

Written that way the thesis is stronger than one where the statistics had simply worked, because it says *how* the claim was established rather than asserting it. Draft that paragraph early — it is the spine of the general introduction and it is the question an examiner will ask first.

### "Atlantic–European–Mediterranean Region" — and Paper 3

The equatorial β-plane work sits outside the named region unless it is framed as equatorial Atlantic. One sentence in the introduction resolves it, but the sentence has to exist: either the chapter is equatorial Atlantic and therefore inside the region, or it is a mechanism chapter whose applicability to the region is the point.

The second framing is the honest one and the stronger one. Say it deliberately rather than hoping nobody asks.

`Primary · Scientific Reports, in review · Ch. 1 · Objective 1`

Four DJF SST regimes from SOM + k-means on HadISST 1950–2024, reorganising Atlantic–European circulation through weather-regime frequency, EKE, momentum flux and LWA. R3 lifts Greenland Blocking from 10% to 27%. R4 is post-2021 and absent from the prior record.

**CCM is carved out entirely** — a data mismatch in the inputs, and the material moves to Paper 4. What remains is the lead-lagged composite: R3 significant at eleven consecutive leads from 10 to 30 days, peak z = +4.06 at 18 days, FDR-surviving, pattern correlation 0.70–0.89. Plus out-of-sample forecast skill, R2 and R3 beating climatology for NAO beyond 30 days under leave-one-winter-out regression. Daily SST regime index reproduces 79.9% of monthly labels.

## A note on the ageing counters

The dates in this register came from the portfolio tracker of 2 August, and where nothing has been written down since, the counters read as though nothing has happened. That was wrong about Paper 3 — weekly meetings have been running throughout, and the counter said 23 days.

The counter is only as honest as its inputs. In the built site, `updated` has to be set from real events, and it must be trivial to change, or it will quietly report stalls that are not stalls. Worth Bernadette knowing when she builds it: **the failure mode of an ageing counter is confident wrongness**, and the fix is making the update cheap enough that it actually happens.
